!TubeSearch - browse YouTube on RISC OS                         @VERSION@
===================================================================

TubeSearch shows YouTube's videos in a desktop window, with their pictures,
and plays the one you click in Reel (riscos-ffmpeg), which uses the
Raspberry Pi's hardware decoder through ReelHWAccel. It is not made by
or connected with YouTube or Google.

It needs:
  - RISC OS 5.28 or later (the AcornSSL module, for https);
  - SharedUnixLibrary 1.16 or later and ARMEABISupport (PackMan);
  - Reel 0.1.27 or later: open the directory that has !Reel in it once,
    so the Filer has seen it (that sets up the *Reel command TubeSearch uses);
  - a desktop of 32 thousand or 16 million colours (the pictures are
    plotted without a colour table).

Using it
--------
Double-click !TubeSearch: its icon (a telly) appears on the icon bar. Click it
to open the window. Type what you want to watch in the box at the top
and press Return (or click Search). You can also paste a YouTube address
(youtube.com/watch?v=..., youtu.be/...): TubeSearch plays that video and shows
the ones related to it.

In the window:
  Select on a video     plays it in Reel
  Adjust on a video     shows the videos related to it
  Select on the         shows that channel's videos
    channel's name
  Select on a channel   (in search results) its videos
  More (the last tile)  fetches the next part of the list
  Back                  the page before (up to 24 are kept)
  Subscriptions         the newest videos of the channels you follow
                        (Adjust: the channels); see Subscriptions below
  Refresh (or F5)       the page on screen fetched again: new results, a
                        channel's newest videos, or a live stream that has
                        started since
  Menu                  Play, Related, Channel, Subscribe, Back, Refresh,
                        Quality, Frame rate and Sound

Subscriptions
-------------
TubeSearch keeps its own list of channels you follow, on this machine; no
YouTube account is involved. Subscribe on a channel's menu (or a video's,
for its channel) adds one; Unsubscribe takes it off.

  Subscriptions (the toolbar button, or the icon bar menu)
                        the newest videos from all of them, newest first;
                        ones since you last looked are marked New
  Adjust on Subscriptions, or My channels on the icon bar menu
                        the channels themselves (Select opens one)

To bring in the channels you follow on YouTube, drop a list of them on
TubeSearch's window or icon bar icon: Google Takeout's subscriptions.csv
(takeout.google.com, YouTube and YouTube Music, subscriptions), or an
export from NewPipe or FreeTube. Channels already there aren't added
twice; their names are brought up to date.

The videos come from each channel's YouTube feed, one channel at a time
(the status line counts them), so a long list takes a while. A channel
whose feed can't be read is looked at on its page instead. The list is
kept in Choices:TubeSearch.Subscriptions.

Beside Search, the Uploaded filter (click it, or its menu button) limits
searches to videos uploaded Today, This week, This month or This year, or
Any time. Changing it searches again with the same words; Back returns to
the results before. Config's search_date sets where it starts.

Menu on the icon bar icon has Info (the version), Search...,
Subscriptions, My channels, Quality, Frame rate, Sound and Quit.

Quality (on the menus) is the tallest video asked for: 360p, 480p,
720p or 1080p (the default). Frame rate is the most frames a second: 30
(the default) or 60. TubeSearch asks YouTube for H.264 video, which the
Pi decodes in hardware up to 1080p, and AAC sound. If a video has nothing
within both, it keeps to the Quality and takes the slowest frame rate.

Quality, Frame rate, Sound and the Uploaded filter are remembered: they're
saved in Choices:TubeSearch.Choices as you choose them, and used the next
time TubeSearch starts (over the Config file's settings).

Many videos now have dubbed sound tracks besides the original (YouTube
adds some itself). Sound on the menus chooses: My language plays the
track in your language (Config's language setting, en-GB at first) if
the video has one, and the original otherwise; Original always plays
what the video was made with. The status line says which it is.

Videos are played as HLS: YouTube's playlists of short pieces, each
fetched on its own. YouTube can slow down, or refuse part way, the two
long streams it otherwise gives (DASH), which shows as buffering or
"forbidden" in Reel. If HLS ever gives trouble, stream_format = dash in
Config goes back to those. Either way Reel gets the video and the sound
separately, as yt-dlp would give them.

Reel is given a small file in <Wimp$ScrapDir> (TubeSearchPlay1 to 4) in the
same form as yt-dlp's -j output: the title, the two addresses and the
headers. Reel's own Media info window shows what's playing.

When YouTube won't give TubeSearch playable addresses
-----------------------------------------------------
TubeSearch first asks as YouTube's visionOS app, whose addresses play as
they are. When YouTube won't serve that app ("Sign in to confirm you're
not a bot", say), TubeSearch asks again as YouTube's TV app. Its
addresses carry challenges that only YouTube's own JavaScript answers, so
TubeSearch fetches YouTube's player and has yt-dlp's solver answer them in
QuickJS, a JavaScript engine built into tscli.

That runs as a task of its own ("TubeSearch solver" in the Task manager),
so the desktop carries on meanwhile. The first video after YouTube updates
its player (every week or two) takes a while: on a Raspberry Pi 4 perhaps
half a minute, and up to about 110 MB of memory, freed afterwards. The
prepared player is kept (in <Wimp$ScrapDir>.TubeSearch.Players), so the
videos after it take a second or two. The status line says what's
happening; choosing another video stops it.

The solver scripts keep themselves up to date: once a day TubeSearch
reads a small file in its GitHub repository saying which version yt-dlp
currently uses, and downloads that version from yt-dlp's own releases,
checking it against the hashes in that file. If a new version doesn't
work, TubeSearch goes back to its own copy. ejs_update = never in Config
turns this off.

Through a proxy
---------------
On some connections (mobile broadband, shared addresses) YouTube often
asks you to sign in to prove you're not a bot. Going through a web proxy
elsewhere (one that accepts CONNECT, such as Squid or Tinyproxy on a
server of your own) makes YouTube see that network instead.

Set the http_proxy variable, in lower case, with http:// and the port;
the best place is your boot sequence (PreDesk), or type it before
playing anything:
  *Set http_proxy http://host:port/
  *Set http_proxy http://user:password@host:port/
  *Unset http_proxy                   (no proxy again)
In a password, write @ as %40, : as %3A, / as %2F, | as %7C and < as
%3C (*Set changes | and < itself).

TubeSearch and Reel (through FFmpeg) both read it, and TubeSearch reads it
the same way Reel does, so they always agree. They must: YouTube's video
addresses only work from the connection that asked for them. TubeSearch
reads it again before each search or video, as Reel does each time it
starts. A value Reel would ignore (no http://, no port) is reported, and
then neither uses a proxy. If the proxy refuses or can't be reached, the
status line says so.

Hosts named in no_proxy (*Set no_proxy name,name...) are reached
directly. Don't name youtube.com or googlevideo.com there unless you name
both: the video addresses come from one and play from the other.

When it stops working
---------------------
YouTube changes what it expects from its apps every few months. TubeSearch
copies the requests that yt-dlp and NewPipe make (October 2026). If
videos stop playing ("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", or "no
H.264 streams"), newer client names and versions can be put in the
Config file inside !TubeSearch (or a copy in Choices:TubeSearch.Config, which wins)
without a new TubeSearch; the file says where to find them.

The addresses YouTube gives work only from the internet connection that
asked for them, and for a few hours.

tscli
-------
!TubeSearch.tscli is the same YouTube code for a TaskWindow, to try things
and find faults (and the JavaScript helper):
  tscli search <words>          the results, as text
  tscli related <video ID>
  tscli channel <UC... ID>
  tscli play <video ID> [lines] [file]   writes Reel's file
  tscli -dump <directory> ...   keeps YouTube's raw answers
  tscli -config <file> ...      reads settings as Config does
  tscli -client tv play <ID>    the TV app (and the solver) only
  tscli update                  checks for newer solver scripts now
  tscli -date week search ...   search with the Uploaded filter
  tscli -proxy <URL>|none ...   a proxy instead of http_proxy
  tscli -sound original ...     the sound track: mine, original or a language
  tscli -fps 60 ...             the most frames a second
  tscli feed UC... [UC...]      those channels' newest videos
  tscli import <file>           the channels in a subscriptions list
